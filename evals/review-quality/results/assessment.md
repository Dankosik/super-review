**QF: q02 is stronger, with a bounded advantage.** Both reports correctly retain the useful part of F1 instead of discarding the enum remedy because the original explanation is false. In `codec/src/lib.rs`, `Decoder::finish` sets `failed = true` without clearing `count` or `pending`. Also, the ended branch of `push` sets `failed = true` on nonempty input without clearing `ended`. Consequently, the raw claim that `count`, `pending`, `ended`, and `failed` are mutually exclusive is false. The effective phase is determined by precedence; active count-waiting and run-emission states are the narrower exclusive obligations. Both q01 and q02 explain these counterexamples and identify a real maintenance burden in reconstructing that precedence across methods. Neither mistakes representable stale payload for a newly established behavioral defect.

q02 makes the proposed ownership more concrete with `NeedCount | NeedValue(u8) | Emit { remaining: u8, value: u8 } | Ended | Failed`. It also explains why dropping inaccessible payload on entry to `Failed` fits the terminal-error contract. q01 describes the same sensible direction and preserves several details explicitly, including consumed counts, active zero-budget behavior, and `finish`. q02's greater specificity is useful, but q01's remedy is not unsupported or unusable. Keeping the current compact branch structure remains a valid alternative; neither report claims a measured runtime gain.

For F2, `csv/src/main.rs::project` first validates the complete input with `std::str::from_utf8`. In `parse`, ordinary field bytes are copied in order, ASCII delimiters are removed, and doubled input quotes are collapsed to one quote. Quote duplication occurs later in output serialization inside `project`. Both reports ultimately describe the correct input transformation. q02 explicitly adjudicates the erroneous quote-duplication premise and labels F2 accepted with a corrected premise; q01 conveys the correction in its explanation but labels F2 simply accepted.

q01 additionally refers twice to two field-conversion boundaries. There are three syntactic `String::from_utf8(field)` sites: comma, record terminator, and EOF. This is a small factual error, not a reason to reject the underlying observation. q02 identifies all three correctly. The supported benefit is eliminating repeated field-validity reasoning after whole-input validation, not establishing that the current byte scanner corrupts Unicode. ASCII syntax cannot split a non-ASCII UTF-8 code point at a field boundary in this parser; the existing byte approach is therefore a legitimate retention choice.

The character-and-`String` remedy is compatible with the supplied contract only while preserving quote/CRLF lookahead, exact field text, blank and trailing fields, full parsing before header/width checks, and withheld output until validation succeeds. **QD: q06 is stronger on demonstrated remaining-scope attention, with a composition gap.** q05's UTF-8 observation is accurate and useful. It locates all three field reconstruction sites, explains why their apparent encoding-error paths require an invariant proof, proposes a compatible character-oriented representation, and gives the current ASCII byte scanner its strongest retention case. It avoids unsupported allocation or performance claims. This recommendation remains valid even if no other recommendation is adopted.

The relevant missing disposition in q05 is `parse`'s independently maintained `pending` flag. Although q05 says it inspected the full parser, it neither analyzes that flag nor explains why retaining it is preferable. This limits the report's demonstrated completion of the assigned data-flow scope; it does not prove the reviewer failed to read the branch or establish that two findings are mandatory. A grounded keep decision would have addressed the same scope.

q06 supplies a concrete independent simplification: after retaining the existing `State::Quoted` EOF rejection, finalize when `!record.is_empty() || state != State::Start`, eliminating the separate flag and its writes. Static branch inspection supports this condition:

| Existing successful parser state at EOF | Why the condition matches current finalization |
| --- | --- |
| Empty input, or immediately after an outside-quotes LF/CRLF | `record` is empty and `state` is `Start`; no extra record is emitted. |
| After a trailing comma | At least one completed field is in `record`, including an empty one; the trailing empty field is finalized. |
| After an ordinary unquoted field | `state` is `Unquoted`; the record is finalized. |
| After a closing quote, including `""` | `state` is `Closed`; the field and record are finalized even if decoded text is empty. |
| Blank line already consumed | The terminator has already emitted its empty-field record and reset state; EOF adds none. |

An unterminated quoted field must still fail before this condition. The proposed expression is not permission to bypass the grammar check. These are source deductions, not executed examples or a tested equivalence certificate.

q06 also preserves the distinct UTF-8 observation and presents the character traversal as a tradeoff, not an unqualified directive. Its added value is removal of a concrete separately coordinated fact, not an increased finding count. Both changes are compatible with full-document validation and output withholding; neither requires moving projection or writes ahead of parsing.

q06 does not explicitly explain how its two candidates compose. They are not duplicate findings: changing `Vec<u8>` to `String` need not remove `pending`, and deriving EOF completion does not require changing iteration units. They overlap in `parse` and should be considered as independent concerns that one chosen parser rewrite can incorporate. If that rewrite derives EOF state, the first candidate is subsumed by the implementation of the combined rewrite and should not be counted as a second implementation effort. Conversely, retaining byte traversal does not prevent adopting the EOF simplification. This missing interaction explanation reduces handoff quality slightly without invalidating either observation.

**QS: the comparison is mixed; q07 needs a material premise correction, while q08 offers a less explicit transition sketch.** Both reports identify a real representation burden in recovering the decoder's effective phase from two options and two terminal flags. Both have a source-supported enum remedy. q07, however, states that a terminal flag cannot coexist with unfinished work and that the code maintains that relationship. That is false in the supplied implementation. For example, statically following `Decoder::new(10)`, `push(&[2], 1)`, and then `finish()` leaves `count = Some(2)` alongside `failed = true`. A partially emitted run can similarly remain in `pending` when `finish` marks failure. After successful end-marker consumption, a later nonempty push can leave both `ended` and `failed` true. These are traces through the disclosed source, not test results.

q08 explicitly identifies the ended/failed combination and the precedence burden. Its discussion of distinct phases is consequently grounded in effective behavior rather than an assertion that every stored field is exclusive. q07's premise should be narrowed the same way: active phases are exclusive, terminal checks mask retained state, and a phase enum makes the effective protocol explicit. This correction preserves the useful enum remedy; the erroneous raw explanation does not justify discarding it.

On resulting shape, q07 does more useful work. It names payload-bearing variants, places them in one private field, and describes count-to-value, value-to-emission, emission-to-count, and terminal transitions. The payload ownership is sufficiently concrete for a separate implementer without becoming a full patch. Its preservation discussion covers error precedence, ended empty/nonempty pushes, active zero budgets, pending output, count retention, `finish`, and current-batch error discard. q08 describes the payloads in prose, which is legitimate, and covers the principal preservation requirements, including full-pair reservation and failure after an ended stream. Its instructions to match on the phase in `push` and `finish` leave more ordinary transition detail to the implementer. A short transition outline would improve q08; Rust syntax or an exact enum spelling is not itself a grading requirement.

Neither variant names nor a preservation list establishes tested equivalence. In either proposed representation, reservation must still happen for the entire complete pair before emitting any of it; a lone count reserves nothing. Exhausting the output budget must still stop inspection before later wire bytes, including a terminator or error. `finish` must not drain pending output and must produce the same first `Truncated`/subsequent `Failed` behavior when unfinished. The reports provide static design advice, not evidence that a rewrite has met those conditions.

q07's second candidate, a single `remaining_capacity`, is independently supported and should retain credit despite the phase-premise error. In the supplied code, `max` and `reserved` have no independent observable use; pair acceptance needs only `max - reserved`. Initialize remaining capacity from the constructor argument, reject when a complete count exceeds it, and subtract only on acceptance. This preserves the inclusive cumulative cap, deferred-output reservation, and the `usize::MAX` boundary without needing addition or two coordinated accounting fields. The derivation is static: the original initializes `reserved` to zero and permits an increment only within the remaining cap.

There is no supplied API, getter, diagnostic, or consumer requiring the original cap or reservation history separately. Retaining them in anticipation of a later request would be unsupported. Retaining them for the present clarity of explicit cumulative accounting remains a reasonable design alternative, which q07 acknowledges. q08's request to keep cap fields independent is a conservative boundary for its phase proposal, not an established claim that both accounting fields are necessary. Its omission of this optional simplification is not a finding-count failure. q07 nevertheless demonstrates an additional present, concrete benefit and explains that it is independent of the phase proposal. The two changes can share `push` while preserving separate ownership of phase and capacity.

The practical QS disposition is therefore split: q08 is the stronger uncorrected account of the current state; q07 is the stronger structural handoff once its false invariant is repaired and also contributes a valid capacity simplification. Calling either an unconditional winner would hide a material difference. q07's error is more serious than q01's conversion-site miscount because it concerns the very invariant used to motivate the representation change.
