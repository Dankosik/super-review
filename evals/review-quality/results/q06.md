## C-q06-1: `pending` duplicates the parser's record state

- **Change anchor:** Frozen `src/main.rs`, `parse`: `let mut pending = false;`, its assignments in the quoted, comma, terminator, quote-start, and unquoted branches, and `if pending { record.push(...); records.push(record); }` at EOF.
- **Basis:** `rust.data.make-transformations-visible`.
- **Observation:** `pending` is maintained beside `record` and `state` solely to decide whether EOF completes an unterminated record. Its value has to be coordinated with every branch that consumes a byte. At EOF, the already assembled state carries that decision: a nonempty `record` means a comma has begun another field, while a non-`Start` state means a field has begun. The separate flag makes readers verify the same transition twice, especially for an empty quoted field, trailing comma, and final record terminator.
- **Remedy:** Derive the EOF decision from `!record.is_empty() || state != State::Start` and remove the `pending` writes. This makes the completion condition visible where it is used and removes one piece of synchronized state. The strongest keep case is that a named flag states intent succinctly; here its five update sites obscure which structural states actually require finalization. No additional owner is needed.
- **Preserve:** An entirely empty input has no record; a final LF/CRLF adds no record; a blank line has already emitted one empty-field record; a trailing comma and `""` both complete an unterminated record. The proposed condition follows those supplied branches, but no source was executed, so equivalence remains unverified.

- **Basis:** `rust.data.make-transformations-visible`.
- **Observation:** The parser copies complete input bytes in order and removes or replaces only ASCII CSV syntax (`""` becomes `"`). The strongest keep case is that byte positions and ASCII delimiter matching are simple in the current parser; switching iteration units adds lookahead details and needs a careful equivalence review. The observation stands, while the exact replacement shape remains a design tradeoff for the orchestrator rather than an implementation directive.
